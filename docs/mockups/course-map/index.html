The Vim Wilds · design review

Course Map mockups

Four designs for the Contents sheet, plus two ways to arrange the Practice tab. Every mockup draws the same learner: in Unit 5, Lesson 2, with Units 1–4 finished. Titles, boards, guides and paintings are the real content, read from content/ and assets/. The phones are live: switch tabs, open units and lessons. A tap on anything else shows what it would open.

A · Index B · Trail C · Themed contents D · Drill-in Practice tab: P1 vs P2 Reference tab

Shared by all four

Three tabs: Course (the default), Practice and Reference. The sheet opens on Course, scrolled to the unit you're in, which is expanded down to your activity.

Any unit opens in place

Every unit expands to its lessons and activities. Tapping an activity goes straight there. Nothing is locked; a soft note still says when a unit builds on one you haven't finished.

Where things went

  • Story: Prologue at the top of Course, "Replay chapter" on each finished unit, Epilogue at the end.
  • Mastery map: it now is the Practice tab.
  • Free practice: one Scratchpad row at the bottom of Practice.
  • Field notes: moved to Reference, since they're reading, not drills.
  • Commands by unit: on each unit, and as a compact grid in Reference.
Pragmatic

A · Index

Idea

A calm, dense table of contents with no art. It keeps today's arc headings and type, cleaned up, with one line per unit.

Progress reads as

A ✓ on finished units, a "Here" pill and a lesson bar on the current one, and a lesson count on the rest.

Trade-offs

  • Fastest to scan, and the smallest build.
  • Uses none of the world, so it feels like a utility.
Gamified

B · Trail

Idea

The course as a path. Each unit is a node with its guide's portrait, and each arc is a milestone. The line takes each unit's world colour, which is the only sign of a world. Finished nodes carry their chapter painting.

Progress reads as

A solid trail behind you and a dashed one ahead. Guides you've met are in colour, the ones ahead are greyed. The current node glows, and the next one shows the hook the last chapter left.

Trade-offs

  • The most "journey" of the four, and the characters do the work.
  • The trail rail costs width, so activity rows wrap more at 360.
  • Three guides teach two units each (Cinder, Luma, Tock), so a few faces repeat.
Middle ground

C · Themed contents

Idea

A's structure and density. Each unit row carries a slim strip of its own board. When a unit opens, its row turns into a banner of that board, with the title on a scrim and the guide beside it, and the lessons below.

Progress reads as

A ✓ on finished units, and a lesson bar in the banner of the open unit. Units ahead have desaturated strips, so the colour spreads as you go.

Trade-offs

  • Scans like a table of contents but feels like the Wilds.
  • The banner doubles as your "you are here".
  • The build needs small board thumbnails; today's boards are about 500 KB each.
Two levels

D · Drill-in Chosen · revised

Idea

The Course tab is a list of units in C's slim rows. Opening one slides to a full page for that unit: a short board hero, its guide, then lessons and activities. The hero collapses into a sticky bar as you scroll, so "‹ All units" and the unit's title stay on screen. A pager steps to the previous or next unit.

Progress reads as

On the unit page: a lesson bar, and a story line (how a finished chapter ended, or the hook the previous one left). In the list: ✓, "here" with the lesson, or a lesson count.

Behaviour

  • Opens on your unit's page; the list is one tap back and lands on your unit.
  • Esc or back steps to the list first; a second press closes the map. × always closes.
  • The hero is about 140px on a 740px phone, 176px on the tallest.
Practice tab

P1 · By unit  vs  P2 · By next step

Shared

Sessions first: Mixed review as the main card, Tool choice beside it. Then Topics, each with its state, a due marker, Drill or Test out, and a pin for mixed review. Free practice shrinks to one Scratchpad row at the bottom. The progress data is sample data; real mastery lives in the learner's browser.

P1 · By unit

Mirrors the Course tab: arcs, then units with "6/7 · 1 due" and a meter. This is how the mastery map is arranged today.

P2 · By next step

Groups by what to do: Due, Learning, Practiced and integrated, and Not started (where Test out lives). Each topic names its unit. It answers "what should I practise?" directly.

Reference tab

One level, nothing hidden

Idea

Every deck is a visible heading with its cards listed underneath, so there is no collapsed "Orientation" to guess at. Field notes join as a deck. A compact grid at the end reaches each unit's command table.

Reads as

"Cards to read. Nothing here is scored." That line, set against Practice's "Every result is kept", is what tells Reference and Mastery apart.

Same in every design

Practice and Reference are shared. Only the Course tab differs between A–D.
